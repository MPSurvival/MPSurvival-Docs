# Screens in the world

Three screens ship with the template: the checkout display, the card terminal and the office computer. All three are children of one class, and two of them have no graph at all.

If you want a screen of your own, this is the page.

---

## `BP_ScreenBase`

Two components and everything else inherited.

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| `ScreenMesh` | The physical screen, the root |
| `Display` | A `WidgetComponent` for the panel itself |
| `ReadPose` | A scene component marking where the camera goes |

What the base already does, so no child has to:

- Carries a `BP_InteractableComponent`, so it has an outline and a prompt.
- On `E`, moves the camera to `ReadPose` and blends the field of view in.
- Hides the crosshair and the interaction prompt.
- Releases whatever you are carrying.
- Freezes movement and look through the controller's ignore counters.
- Shows the mouse cursor.
- Leaves again on `Tab` or a second `E`, undoing all of it.
- Turns off cleanly with `SetPowered(false)`, which hides the display and disables the interactable.

The lean-in is generated by the view motion component, not by a camera actor. Head bob, breathing and look sway keep living on top of it, and the movement dips before it settles instead of easing flatly in.

---

## `BP_ScreenBaseWidget`

The widget side is two functions.

| Function | Contract |
|---|---|
| `SetHost(NewHost)` | Called by the screen. Stores the host actor and calls `BindHost` |
| `BindHost` | Empty in the base. Override it and subscribe to your host |

That pair is what lets the checkout display bind to its checkout without the base knowing what a checkout is.

There is a matching `BeginRead(Reader)` on the actor, also empty in the base, called when someone leans in. Override it if your screen has to wake up, not simply be looked at.

## Building one

1. Create a child of `BP_ScreenBase`.
2. Set `ScreenMesh` to your monitor mesh.
3. Set `Display.WidgetClass` to your widget.
4. Position and scale `Display` so it sits on the glass.
5. Move `ReadPose` until leaning in frames the screen the way you want.

`BP_CheckoutScreen` is exactly that and nothing else: two values in the Details panel and an empty graph. It is the template to copy.

## Sizing the display

This is the part that decides whether your screen looks sharp or soft, and it is worth doing with numbers instead of by eye.

```
DrawSize  ≈  how many screen pixels the panel covers when you lean in
Scale     =  physical width in cm  /  DrawSize
```

A widget rendered at 1024 across and covering 1411 pixels on the monitor is being magnified, and no amount of anti-aliasing hides that.

Then lay the widget out at a fixed design size inside a scale box, so changing `DrawSize` re-renders rather than re-flows.

Two more settings on the widget component:

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Blend Mode | `Transparent` | `Masked` ghosts under temporal anti-aliasing |
| Window Focusable | off | A focusable window steals keyboard input |

And on every button inside: **Is Focusable off**, or `Tab` walks a focus rectangle around the screen instead of leaving it.

## Positioning the panel on the mesh

Two things learned doing it three times:

**A mesh's bounds do not tell you where a face is.** The bounding box of a monitor includes its stand, so reading `max X` off the bounds and calling it the front of the glass puts the panel in the wrong place. Measure the glass.

**A real screen's bezel is not symmetrical.** The shipped POS display has 1.6 cm at the top and 3.3 at the bottom. Centre the panel in the mesh and you get something that looks like a laptop, not a till.

The panel position and scale are Details panel values. If you re-export the mesh, they are fields to nudge, not a calculation to redo.

## The one exception to leaning in

`BP_DrawerWidget`, the change readout on the till drawer, is in **screen space** rather than world space, so it stays readable while you move your head over the drawer.

It still belongs to the drawer, and it still lives in the `World/` widget folder, because the question is whose information it is and not how it is drawn.
